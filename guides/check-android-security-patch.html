How to check your Android security patch level (and why it matters)

One date in your settings tells you whether your phone has the latest security fixes.

Updated 17 October 2026

Every month Google publishes a list of security problems fixed in Android. Phone makers bundle those fixes into a security update, and your phone shows the date of the most recent one it has: the security patch level. The older that date, the more known holes are still open.

Where to find it

PhoneWhere to look
Most phones (Android 12+)Settings › About phone › Android version › Android security update
SamsungSettings › About phone › Software information › Android security patch level
PixelSettings › Security & privacy › System & updates › Security update
Other brandsSearch for “security” or “patch” in the search field at the top of Settings.

What the date tells you

Less than 3 months old
Up to date. Nothing to do.
3 to 6 months old
Check for an update. Many phones get one every month or every quarter.
More than 6 months old
Your phone may no longer get updates. Check the maker's support period.

The Google Play system update, on the same screen, is a separate thing: Google updates parts of Android through the Play Store. It helps, but doesn't replace the security update from your phone maker.

How to install the latest security update

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and charge the phone to at least 50%.
  2. Open Settings › System › Software update – on Samsung Settings › Software update › Download and install, on Pixel Settings › Security & privacy › System & updates.
  3. Install the update and restart when asked. The fixes only take effect after a restart.

Many people postpone that restart for days or weeks. Our other app, RebootMonitor, keeps track of when your phone was last restarted and reminds you when it's been too long.

When your phone no longer gets updates

  • Look up the support period: Samsung, Google and others publish until when each model gets security updates.
  • Keep your apps up to date – app updates still arrive through the Play Store and fix problems in the apps themselves.
  • Be extra careful with APK files and links, and avoid apps with accessibility or device admin access you don't need.
  • Plan a replacement for a phone you use for banking or work.

Frequently asked questions

How often should my phone get a security update?

Many recent phones get one every month, others every quarter. If your patch level is more than six months old, your phone may have reached the end of its support period.

Is the Google Play system update the same as the security update?

No. The Google Play system update refreshes some Android components through the Play Store. The Android security update comes from your phone maker and covers much more.

Do I need to restart after a security update?

Yes. A security update is only active after the phone has restarted. Until then, the old, vulnerable code keeps running.
