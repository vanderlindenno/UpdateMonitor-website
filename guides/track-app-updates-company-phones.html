How to track app updates on company Android phones (for IT admins)

Which apps changed on a user's phone, when, and with which permissions? Options with and without device management.

Updated 17 October 2026

A user calls: “the app doesn't work any more since this morning”. To help, you want to know what changed on that phone – which apps were updated, from which version, and whether anything new got installed. Here are your options, from fully managed to no management at all.

Option 1: Android Enterprise with an MDM/EMM

With a device management system (MDM or EMM) and Android Enterprise, you control apps centrally through managed Google Play:

  • App inventory per device, with installed versions.
  • Update policies: update automatically, only on Wi-Fi or while charging, or postpone updates for a while.
  • Approved apps only: block installs from unknown sources in the work profile.
  • Permission policies: grant or deny permissions centrally.

Limits: the inventory shows the current state, not always a history of changes; personal apps on a BYOD phone (outside the work profile) are deliberately invisible to IT; and a full MDM is a big step for a small organisation.

Option 2: without device management

Many small organisations, schools and clubs hand out phones without an MDM. Then you depend on what the user can show you – and Android itself keeps no update history.

That's where UpdateMonitor helps. Installed on the phone, it keeps a local history of every app update, install and removal, with old and new versions and permission changes:

  1. The user installs UpdateMonitor from the Play Store; the history starts right away and also shows recent updates from before the install.
  2. Optionally turn on warnings for new permissions and apps installed outside the Play Store.
  3. When there's a problem, the user filters the list – for example the last 7 days – and taps Export.
  4. The user sends you the CSV (for a spreadsheet) or PDF (a readable report) by mail or chat.

Privacy: why this works for BYOD

UpdateMonitor has no internet permission. Nothing is sent to IT, to us, or to anyone else; the history stays on the phone. The user decides what to share, and when. That makes it acceptable on personal phones where a full MDM would be too intrusive – and it's free, without ads or tracking.

Tips for your helpdesk

  • Ask for the export of the last 7 days as the first step for “it stopped working” tickets.
  • Compare the time of the problem with the update times – usually one update stands out.
  • Check the Source column for apps that didn't come from the Play Store.
  • Check the security patch level too; see how to check the security patch.

Frequently asked questions

Can IT see app updates on a phone without an MDM?

Not remotely. Android keeps no update history and sends nothing to IT. With an app like UpdateMonitor the history is kept on the phone and the user can export it to send to the helpdesk.

Does UpdateMonitor send data to the company?

No. UpdateMonitor has no internet permission, so it can't send anything. The user exports and shares a CSV or PDF report manually.

Can I postpone app updates on managed phones?

Yes, with Android Enterprise and managed Google Play most MDM systems let you set update policies, including postponing updates for a period or only updating on Wi-Fi.
