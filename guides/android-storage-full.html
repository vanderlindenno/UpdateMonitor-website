Android storage full? What to do – and why app updates fail

Why a nearly full phone stops installing updates, and the quickest ways to win back space.

Updated 17 October 2026

Your phone says Storage space running out, the Play Store shows Can't download app or an update hangs on Pending. In almost every case the reason is the same: there isn't enough free space left for the update to be downloaded and installed.

Why app updates fail when storage is low

An update isn't installed in place. Android first downloads the new version, keeps the old one until the new one is ready, and often needs extra room to optimise the app. For a while an update can take two to three times the size of the app.

A 300 MB app can therefore need close to 1 GB of free space for a moment – even though it only grows by a few megabytes. That's why updates fail long before your storage is at 100%.

Rule of thumb: keep at least 10% of your storage, or 2–3 GB, free. Below that, larger updates start to fail and the phone can feel slower.

See what's using your storage

  1. Open Settings › Storage. On Samsung phones it's Settings › Battery and device care › Storage.
  2. Wait until the categories have been counted: apps, images, videos, audio, documents, system, other.
  3. Tap a category to see the largest items first.

The System part can't be reduced; it's Android itself. Focus on apps, videos and downloads.

7 ways to free up space

Clear app cache

Open Settings › Apps, pick a large app such as a browser or social media app, then Storage › Clear cache. Safe: you stay logged in.

Remove apps you don't use

Sort the app list by size. An app you haven't opened in months can go – you can always reinstall it from the Play Store.

Archive apps

Recent Android versions can archive apps: the app is removed but its icon and data stay. In the Play Store: profile icon › Settings › General › Automatically archive apps.

Move photos and videos

Videos are usually the biggest item. Back them up to a computer or cloud service, then remove them from the phone.

Empty the Downloads folder

Old PDFs, APK files and attachments pile up. Open the Files app, go to Downloads and sort by size.

Offline media

Downloaded music, podcasts, maps and streaming episodes can take gigabytes. Remove what you've already listened to or watched.

Clear chat media

Messaging apps keep every photo, video and voice message. Most have their own storage manager under the app's settings.

Avoid “cleaner” or “booster” apps. They mostly do what Android already does for free, and many show ads or ask for far more access than they need. If you're unsure what an app has access to, see special app access explained.

After freeing up space

  1. Open the Play Store, tap your profile icon and choose Manage apps & device.
  2. Tap Update all, or update the app that failed.
  3. Still stuck on Pending? Restart the phone and try again.

Frequently asked questions

How much free space does an app update need?

Usually two to three times the size of the app for a short moment, because Android keeps the old version until the new one is installed. Keeping at least 10% of your storage free avoids most problems.

Is it safe to clear an app's cache?

Yes. The cache only holds temporary files the app can download or create again. Clearing storage or data is different: that resets the app and may log you out.

Why is my storage full when I don't have many photos?

Apps, their caches, chat media, offline music and maps, and the Downloads folder often take more space than photos. The Storage screen in Settings shows which category is largest.
